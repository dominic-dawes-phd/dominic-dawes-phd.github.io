I am a PhD student at UMBC. My goal is to build algorithms and systems for autonomous robots and embodied agents that reason about and interact with the real world. My research lies at the intersection of collaborative robotics, machine learning, and computer vision. I focus on developing methods and principles of perception, decision-making, and life-long learning to deploy general-purpose robot autonomy in the wild.

Before joining UMBC, I spent a year working as a Computer Vision / Machine Learning Scientist at the Johns Hopkins Applied Physics Laboratory (APL) in Laurel, MD, where I still work while pursuing my PhD part-time. Previously, I attended the University of Pittsburgh for my undergraduate degree and Duke University for my Master's in Biomedical Engineering.

Research interests:
- Fleet / swarm command and control (C2)
- Collaborative AI for robot decision making
- Robot control and planning
